Remote work has changed how many companies operate. A 2023 survey of 1,200 employees across three countries found that 62% preferred hybrid arrangements over full-time office work. The study also noted that people who worked from home at least three days a week cut their average commute by 45 minutes a day. That is a significant shift, and it reflects broader changes in how work is organized.

The same survey also revealed problems. Managers said onboarding new hires had become harder, and 38% of respondents felt less connected to their colleagues. How far these results apply depends on industry, seniority, and household situation. Some critics argue the benefits are overstated. Others contend the problems are largely a matter of poor management.

The findings point to a need for thoughtful policy design. Companies that set clear expectations, invest in deliberate onboarding, and hold regular in-person occasions tend to report better outcomes. Remote work is not one model but a set of choices, and those choices matter.
